# Verb Tense Consistency

> WAIS demo content aligned to MATATAG Quarter 1 learning goals. This is an original classroom learning aid, not official curriculum text.

## Learning goal

By the end of this lesson, you can explain **consistent tense**, use it in a clear example, and apply the idea in a short task.

![Concept guide for Verb Tense Consistency](images/concept-guide.webp)

## Key idea

`consistent tense` is the focus of this lesson. Writers keep time relationships clear by choosing consistent verb tenses.

Connect the example to the key idea, then explain the connection in your own words.

## Worked example

Yesterday we walked and talked, not walked and talk.

Notice how the example supports the key idea instead of simply naming it. Ask: Which detail is evidence for the main idea, and how do you know?

![Worked example for Verb Tense Consistency](images/worked-example.webp)

## Guided practice

1. Restate the key idea in your own words.
2. Identify the important information in the worked example.
3. Complete this task: Edit a paragraph with shifting verb tense.
4. Check your answer by returning to the definition and visual.

## Talk and think

- What detail was most useful?
- What is one common mistake someone might make?
- Where could you observe or use this idea at home, in school, or in the community?

## Remember

The goal is not only to memorize **consistent tense**. You should be able to recognize it, explain it, and use it in a new situation.
